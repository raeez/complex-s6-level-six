# First geometric derivation

The target is the toric quotient with periods (q c1,c2), (c3,q c4), not a replacement by a lower-dimensional collapse.

The maximal cones over the two unit triangles have determinant one. The height character is the product of the three affine chart coordinates. Translation by a period shifts vertex labels and preserves this atlas.

For unit periods, the normalized central component has six primitive rays. The hexagon H has that normal fan. Its seven lattice characters give a toric projective embedding. The usual weighted average of the character exponents identifies the nonnegative real part with H. On each boundary edge only two character terms remain. Their ratio is the residual torus character. Opposite edges have translated exponent pairs. Therefore the same residual character gives exactly the stated opposite-edge translation. This supplies an explicit gluing check absent from the short polar-chart argument.

At fixed argument v of q, the logarithmic boundary retains both angular coordinates at each point of H. Crossing a seam by Qn changes the local angle by vn. The combination theta=phi-v Q^{-1}y is invariant. Increasing v by one identifies theta with theta+beta, where beta=Q^{-1}y modulo Z^2. Forgetting normal angles gives [y,theta+vQ^{-1}y]. At an edge the difference vn lies in the collapsed circle. At a vertex the whole angular torus collapses. Thus this boundary formula descends before any cellular replacement.

The finite base cells consist of two vertices, three parallel edges, and one hexagonal face. Integral cycles beta1=e1-e0 and beta2=e0-e2 have displacement Qe1 and Qe2. Edge characters are (-1,1),(-1,0),(0,1). The actual quotient has ranks (2,3,4,2,1). The boundary matrix in degree one has three equal columns (-1,1); opposite face contributions cancel in all higher degrees. The top toric cell survives. This decides against a model that discards all angular fibres.
