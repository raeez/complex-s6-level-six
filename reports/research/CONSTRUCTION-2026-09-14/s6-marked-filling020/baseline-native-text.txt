    The Level-Six Fibre Lattice, Linking Forms, and Torus Gluing

                                                   Raeez Lorgat

                                                      Abstract
           For the lattice Λ = 𝐻1 ( 𝐹 , ℤ) of a smooth complex two-torus fibre in Engel’s fibration, we give
       the two integral monodromy markings and determine the primitive invariant alternating form 𝜉 ,
       up to sign. The distinguished circle class 𝛿0 and invariant covector 𝜓 satisfy

                                                    𝜄𝛿0 𝜉 = −6𝜓 .

       An explicit unimodular basis puts 𝜉 in the form 𝐽 ⊕ 6 𝐽 ; hence its discriminant group is (ℤ/6ℤ) 2 ,
       with a perfect alternating pairing and a six-dimensional irreducible finite Heisenberg representa-
       tion. We also compute the complete Smith form of a two-fibre presentation, including the zero-
       determinant case, and identify its determinant with a denominator-cleared Seifert Euler number.
       For an explicitly constructed effective rank-one Seifert ℂ∗ -bundle with two multiple fibres, we prove
       that the primitive integral Leray carrier is indexed by lcm( 𝑟, 𝑠 ) and has coefficient Δ/gcd( 𝑟, 𝑠 ). The
       full global Seifert class also has a ℤ/gcd( 𝑟, 𝑠 ) component which the coarse transgression does not
       see. We resolve the integral extension hidden by this differential and compute the symmetric torsion
       linking form of every effective two-fibre filling. For arbitrary integral local data, the doubled presen-
       tation has alternating elementary divisors ( 𝑑, | Δ |/ 𝑑 ), where 𝑑 = gcd( 𝑟, 𝑠, 𝑚, 𝑛). These pairings
       have specified, distinct carriers. Engel’s rank-four transgression remains a separate (3, 4) calculation.
       For the same rank-four monodromy, we construct integral chains for gluing three torus bound-
       aries with specified filling maps and their homotopies. The cusp boundary with transport 𝑇∞ℎ has
       𝐻2 = 𝐻3 = ℤ6 ⊕ (ℤ/| ℎ |ℤ) 2 for ℎ ≠ 0. Explicit transported cycles determine its prime-coefficient
       connecting homomorphisms. These boundary groups and the complete gluing differential specify
       the further attachments needed for the singular fibres.
